Design section 11 · Oniit · voice-first agent UI, WebGL orb and live speech

Concierge

Open full screen ↗

A voice concierge for investment firms, family offices and law practices: it moves meetings, reports the pipeline and drafts replies for review. Tap the mic or hold Space: with permission the orb swells with your real voice and transcribes you; without it, a sample request is heard. The orb twists while it thinks and pulses on every word of its reply. Three voices, Chief of Staff, Revenue Officer and Executive Director, recolour the orb and the page.

Tap the mic, or type into “or type…” and press Enter. Turn the sound on to hear replies. Switch voices at the bottom.

Design section 13 · Heronway · brand, design system and motion-led booking site

Heronway: a calm airline, built for motion

Open in a new tab ↗

A fictional boutique airline, designed end to end: the brand, a 22-component design system with its own motion language, and a three-page site built on it.

Self-initiated hospitality concept · fictional brand · brand, design system, motion and front-end build

ScopeWordmark, mark and two mascots; colour, type, space and motion tokens in Day and Night themes; 22 components, 12 of them page-motion patterns; a three-page site: Home, Where we fly, Rewards
BaselineAirline sites lead with fare grids and urgency banners. Motion, where it exists, is decoration that breaks keyboard use and reduced-motion settings
TargetA booking experience where every movement explains something: where the plane goes, what a tier includes, that you are booked
ResultEvery text colour token meets 4.5:1 contrast on all three surfaces in both themes. Every component works by keyboard and drops to instant state changes under reduced motion. One call, Heronway.init(), wires the whole site
Not achievedNo usability testing yet. Booking is a front-end demo with no fares engine behind it. Mascot art is a first pass

The challenge

Budget travel feels hurried by design: countdown timers, upsell walls and a seat map that punishes you for not paying. Heronway asks what “elevated economy” would look like on screen: calm, warm, a little playful, and exact about prices and fine print.

Design decisions

  • Motion has a vocabulary. Three easings named for flight: glide for entrances, soar for anything that travels, hop for Juni’s playful overshoot. Four durations from 150 to 900 ms.
  • Colour tells you where you are. Navy is the brand field and royal is action. Teal, plum, forest and chocolate each give one story its own colour. No yellow or gold anywhere.
  • Two hosts with jobs. Hollis the heron leads informational pages; Juni the fox leads offers and Rewards. Only one animated mascot is in view at a time.
  • Shapes from the cabin. The 32 px radius on hero media and promo bands echoes an aircraft window.
  • Words like a host, not a ticket counter. Sentence case, the benefit first, exact numbers, no exclamation stacks.

Motion, explained

Six moments from the live site. Each one carries information, and each has a still, instant version for people who turn motion off.

How it’s built

Framework-free: tokens compile to CSS variables, components are hw- classes, and one small script wires ripples, reveals, tabs, the booking panel’s airport search, date range and traveller stepper, the route map, the tier table, card flips, counters and page transitions. The Night flight theme swaps every token at once.

Testing

Done: automated browser runs of all three pages at laptop and phone width, checked for layout overflow and script errors. Planned: a five-person test of the booking flow measuring time to a first search, and whether the motion helps people find routes and tier benefits faster than a static version.
