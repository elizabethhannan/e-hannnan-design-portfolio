Research statement

Focus

My research investigates failure modes in large language model alignment that emerge not only from model training, but from the interface designs, user populations, and deployment contexts surrounding those models. I am particularly interested in how sycophantic behavior is amplified by UX patterns, how safety guardrails degrade across non-standard communication modalities, and how adversarial prompts exploit intersections of language, culture, and accessibility.

Drawing on empirical expertise in ESG regulatory compliance, enterprise AI deployment at Fortune 500 scale, and community research conducted with the Deaf & DeafBlind Society of Arizona through the PTK Honors in Action program, I bring grounded domain specificity to questions that standard benchmark evaluations do not yet systematically address.

My future research engages with two distinct but complementary areas: sustainability and ESG transparency as they relate to AI compliance and greenwashing risks; and separately, how AI models interact through an HXO lens with accessibility challenges and adoption barriers across disability communities.

On HXO: The most important part of this AI evolution we are living through is the balance between technology and the humans who use it. If technology cannot be designed to help rather than hurt the human experience, then the value proposition collapses. HXO is my frame for ensuring it does not.

I am open to conversations about Fellowships, Cohorts, Grants, Private Executive Mentorships, and collaborative conversations and research programs aligned with these research directions.
