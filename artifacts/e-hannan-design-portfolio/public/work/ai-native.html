Network Fix Review

AI recommendations an operator can question, correct and teach.

International enterprise cloud, database and network infrastructure software company · client unnamed under NDA · design lead and builder

ScopeAI-recommended network fixes reviewed by operators (accept, edit, reject with a reason), with a change-safety agent
BaselineEvery fix checked by hand; operator corrections not captured
TargetOperators accept correct fixes, catch wrong ones, and every correction becomes a training label
ResultScenario (best case): review time per fix down 40%; 95% of seeded wrong fixes caught before they ran; 100% of corrections saved as labeled examples
Not achievedNo operator study published yet; no live network connected

The challenge

As networks grow, the fixes an AI can suggest grow faster than the number an operator can safely check. An operator who cannot tell a good recommendation from a bad one either rubber-stamps it or ignores the AI, and corrections taught the model nothing. IT and networking issues caused 23% of impactful outages in 2024 (Uptime Institute, 2025), and over 40% of agentic AI projects are expected to be canceled by the end of 2027 (Gartner, 2025).

What the research says

  • Trust has to match reliability, so each recommendation shows its evidence and the track record of its confidence level (Lee & See, 2004).
  • People accept an imperfect algorithm when they can adjust it, so "Edit before applying" sits beside Accept (Dietvorst et al., 2018).
  • Design for when the AI is wrong and for over time: the reject-with-a-reason flow, the abstain state and the model-feedback dashboard (Amershi et al., 2019).

Design tasks

  1. Problem framing and job stories
  2. Task analysis of a single review: read, check evidence, judge confidence, preview, decide, verify
  3. Information hierarchy that follows that order
  4. Heuristic evaluation of every state, including low confidence with high risk and no recommendation at all
  5. Dashboard design for the model team
  6. Telemetry design: each operator goal mapped to a measure and to what it tells the model team

HXO and AXO

The human outcome: an operator who fixes problems fast without breaking users, and who trusts the AI exactly as much as it deserves. For the agent side, every accept, edit, reject and rollback becomes a structured telemetry event and a labeled training example.

Implementation

A diagnosis agent proposes each fix and a separate change-safety agent checks it against what the first cannot see. When they disagree, the item waits for the operator; neither agent can apply a change on its own. A seeded simulation produces 60 days of decisions so every dashboard number comes from one set of records.

Testing

Done: automated browser tests of every decision path at laptop and phone width, in light and dark, plus a numbers check that the dashboard agrees with itself. Planned: a usability test with five network operators measuring time to decide and appropriate reliance.

Human-agent collaboration · agents that hand back control

Agent Hand-Back in Team Chat

An agent working inside a team channel presents what it understood, acts only after a preview, and hands the work back with full context.

Pattern in the HXO Agent Trust and Audit Kit · generic team chat, no product branding · interaction design, design system and build

ScopeOne channel thread: request, understood card, preview-then-apply, approval, hand-back card, undo
BaselineNew pattern, built from kit components shared by design and code
TargetA person always sees what the agent will do before it happens, and can take the work back at any point
ResultBuilt in Figma and in React; automated accessibility checks pass in light and dark
Not achievedNo user test yet

How it works

  1. A person asks in the thread. The agent replies with an Understood card: what it will do, how sure it is, and the evidence.
  2. Preview-then-apply shows the change before it happens; anything that matters waits on an Approval card.
  3. When the agent reaches its limit, a Hand-back card passes the task, intent, confidence, what it ruled out and what happens if no one decides.
  4. An Undo bar stays available after every action.

Regulated B2B workflow · many agents, one accountable person

Claims Coder

Vet SOAP notes and invoices become a traceable claim packet; every code links back to its text.

National pet insurance provider · client unnamed under NDA · design lead and builder

ScopeVet SOAP notes and invoices to a traceable claim packet; 18 agents (13 working, 5 always-on security)
BaselineMost veterinary record systems have no clinical coding built in (Boguslav et al., 2026)
TargetEvery code traceable to its text, page, coding-system version and status; complete packet
Result30 of 30 notes coded exactly on a test set written alongside the agents
Not achievedNo real clinic records tested; no real insurer decisions; reimbursement not predicted

The challenge

Insured pets reached a record 7.03 million in North America at year-end 2024, up 20.9%, and written premium passed $5.2 billion (NAPHIA, 2025). Claims grow with them, but pet insurance has no shared billing code set, so notes and invoices are still read by hand, and adjusters could not see why an automated code was chosen.

Design tasks

Job stories for the adjuster, clinic and owner; task analysis of one claim review; an interaction contract for what agents may and may not do; heuristic evaluation against the guidelines for human-AI interaction (Amershi et al., 2019); plain-language review of every hand-back.

What a person controls

  • An Approval agent approves only clean claims under a limit a person sets; it never denies or pays, and every approval can be undone and is logged as a labeled example.
  • An Emergency agent moves urgent claims to the front of a person's queue; it never approves, pays or denies.
  • Master shutoff, per-agent pause, Stop all, and live uptime, error rate, accuracy and p95 time for every agent.

Testing

Automated browser tests at laptop and phone width in light and dark; a full claim run from upload to approved packet; a flagged claim handed to a person. The 30 of 30 result shows the agents work as designed; it is not evidence of accuracy on real records. Planned: a checked sample of real, consented notes and a usability test with five claims reviewers.

Agent-to-agent orchestration · people step in by exception

Food Rescue Agents

Agents coordinate surplus-food pickups and a household bridge; a person decides only the exceptions.

Feed-A-Friend, a national philanthropic foundation in the food distribution space · design lead and builder

ScopeFive agents coordinate a week of surplus-food pickups and a household bridge; people step in by exception
BaselineA coordinator matched every pickup by phone
TargetAgents handle routine pickups end to end; every escalation reaches a person inside the food-safety window
ResultScenario (best case): 86 of 100 pickups handled by agents end to end; 100% of escalations decided inside the food-safety window; coordinator time per pickup down 70%
Not achievedNo real partners or households contacted; no participant test yet

The challenge

Surplus food spoiled while coordinators made phone calls: every pickup was matched by hand, and the food-safety window closed before a driver was found. Families waiting on benefits needed food now, without retelling their story.

What it shows

  • Agent contracts: intake, safety, matching, partner and dispatch agents each have written limits on what they may do alone.
  • Escalation by exception: each escalation arrives with what the agents tried, the minutes left in the food-safety window and suggested options.
  • Control: pause any one agent or stop them all; deliveries already on the road finish.
  • Trace: every message between agents is logged per pickup.

Spatial & multimodal interfaces · vision work

Spatial Director

Direct a 3D studio office by typing or speaking and pointing; three agents show what they understood before anything changes.

National autonomous vehicle motor company · client unnamed under NDA · design lead and builder

ScopeLanguage, spatial and layout agents; add, move, turn, resize, recolor, remove and agent suggestions
BaselineA wrong guess in a 3D space is easy to miss: the change may happen off-screen or behind another object
TargetThe person always knows what is about to change, and can stop it or take it back
ResultEvery contract rule, the disambiguation question, point-to-place, Stop, undo and the test export pass automated checks on desktop and phone, light and dark
Not achievedFive-task participant sessions not yet run

What the research says

Speech plus pointing is a natural way to direct a spatial display; the open question has always been how the system confirms what "that" and "there" refer to (Bolt, 1980). An agent should act alone only when acting is cheap to undo (Horvitz, 1999).

The interaction contract

  • Add, move, turn, resize and recolor: a preview shows the result, then it applies with undo.
  • Remove: the object turns red and waits for an OK.
  • Agent suggestions: each has a reason and a checkbox; suggestions that add furniture start unchecked.
  • Stop cancels any preview and question and turns the microphone off.

Testing

A five-task usability test is built into the prototype: completion, time on task, clarifying questions, misunderstood requests, stops and undos, and the Single Ease Question, exported as CSV or JSON.
